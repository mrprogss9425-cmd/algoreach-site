Methods

How a relation card is read.

AlgoReach relates stored series to what happened next and shows the result as a card. A card is a description of the stored history with its own uncertainty beside it; it is never a forecast and never a signal. These are the rules every card is read by, in the same words the terminal shows next to each card.

What a card is, and is not

A card names two stored series, a horizon of one, five or twenty sessions and a universe of names, and reports a rank correlation with its band, a placebo band drawn from the same data with the dates shifted, the effective number of trials and the same reading in three volatility regimes.

  • It carries the label UNPROVEN until a pre-registered test says otherwise; the label never leaves the card.
  • A finding is what the owner reads from a card and records with its number; a card alone is not a finding.
  • No weight, threshold or detector is published here; the rules below are how to read, not how to compute.

The seven rules

A rank correlation, not a fit

Spearman's rho ranks both series first, so one wild day cannot make or break the number. It says whether higher x tends to go with higher y, nothing about how much.

The dates are the trials

Cross-sectional cards take one rho per date across the names on that date and average them; the 95% band is a stationary bootstrap over the date series, so neighbouring days that move together are not counted as independent evidence.

A placebo band sits next to every effect

The same statistic is recomputed with the dates shifted so no true alignment survives; an effect inside that placebo band is indistinguishable from chance. 'clears' says whether it stands outside the band AND its own band excludes zero.

N_eff is smaller than n

Overlapping forward returns and clustered days repeat information; the Bartlett effective sample size says how many independent trials the n really amounts to.

Three VIX regimes

The same rho is shown in the low, mid and high India VIX terciles. A relation that flips sign across regimes is reported as such, not averaged away.

Forward and reverse

Where one series is said to lead another, the reverse direction is computed too; a series that merely follows the market shows a stronger reverse card (F-1141a).

Every card is UNPROVEN

A card is a description of the stored history, never a forecast and never a signal. It becomes a finding (an F-number) only when the owner reads it; it enters a model only through a pre-registered fit.

The same seven blocks are served by the terminal's cards API and shown under its Relations panel.

How a move is explained, part by part

For one name and one window, the move is split into parts that add up to it: the market, the sector, the recorded events, the news tone, and what is left unexplained. The market and sector sensitivities are measured on the 250 sessions before the window, so the window never explains itself.

  • A part is a description of the stored history, never a cause and never a forecast.
  • The unexplained part is always shown; it is often the largest.
  • Context from earlier days (macro, flows, commodities) is listed beside the parts as UNPROVEN links.

The interconnection map

Every driver family (macro, flows, derivatives, commodities, cross-market, news tone) is related to what each index and name did one, five and twenty sessions later, and at hour level within the session.

  • Thousands of cells are tested at once, so the false-discovery rate is controlled (Benjamini-Hochberg) before any cell is shown.
  • Each surviving cell must also clear a date-shifted placebo and keep its sign in both halves of the history.
  • A surviving cell is a lead worth testing, labelled UNPROVEN; it is not a finding until a pre-registered test passes.

The price-movement backtest and the model ladder

For each name, the events recorded on earlier days are scored against the next move using only what was known on the day, and the result is shown as a rewind strip with its uncertainty. An event type is scored only once it has enough earlier cases; thin names borrow from their sector.

  • A probability is shown as calibrated only after it passes a fixed gate: enough cases, small calibration error, a calibration slope consistent with one and a forecast score better than the base rate.
  • Models are tried from the simplest (linear) up to a small neural network; a more complex model is kept only if it beats every simpler one that passed.
  • A model that fails is recorded as failed and is never re-tuned until it passes. The first pooled model (H-012) failed and is recorded so.
  • Language models sit upstream, reading text into event types and tone; they are not a rung of the ladder.

Why the record matters more than the card

A card describes the past as stored. The record is what a stated call did at its horizon, scored hit or miss on an append-only ledger. The two are shown apart so that a description is never mistaken for a result.

02 · The market universe

One market is many markets reading each other.

A filing in Mumbai, a rate decision in Washington, a results call at dawn and a headline from Singapore land in the same hour. AlgoReach reads public disclosures, regulators, statistics, news feeds and prices for the markets it covers, and keeps where every item came from.

Coverage today: India and the United States. Each point is a kind of source the engine reads; news feeds are read wherever they are published.

04 · The intelligence engine

Six layers, each one checkable.

From raw disclosure to a scored, risk-aware research signal. Open a layer to see what it means in practice.

  1. 1
    A material event landsA filing, a result, a deal or a regulatory item is detected, classified and attributed to its source document.
  2. 2
    The crowd reacts, and is measuredNarrative sentiment, institutional and retail flow, deals and derivatives positioning are stored as time series, not quoted as a mood.
  3. 3
    Fundamentals and macro set the contextFinancials, valuation, the sector and the macro backdrop frame how similar events played out before.
  4. 4
    A scored signal, with its reasonsDirection, horizon and a calibrated probability, with the evidence, the uncertainty and the counter-case beside it.

  1. Ingestion

    Public exchange disclosures, regulators, news feeds, government statistics and market data, each source with a health status and a licence tag.

    What this means in practice

    A source that refuses automated access is not fetched. Each document carries its URL, fetch time, licence and content hash.

  2. Verification

    Documents are de-duplicated, dated and entity-linked; the data-QA pass flags gaps and incoherence before anything downstream reads them.

    What this means in practice

    Sanity, reconciliation and coherence checks run in shadow and write their findings to the ledger.

  3. Quantitative analysis

    Event studies by type and horizon, factor screens on stored bars, crowd-behaviour series compared with what happened next.

    What this means in practice

    Every study is pre-registered as a hypothesis and tested against a same-name placebo before it is kept.

  4. AI interpretation

    Where a language model drafts a rationale, the deterministic rules it summarises are shown beside it and the model is named.

    What this means in practice

    The model never sets a probability; it explains one.

  5. Scoring

    Research signals in one fixed schema: direction, expected move, horizon, probability, calibration bucket, provenance.

    What this means in practice

    A probability is labelled uncalibrated until enough history exists to calibrate it.

  6. Risk evaluation

    Model portfolios sized by a risk manager with stated caps; exposure, concentration and stress under named scenarios.

    What this means in practice

    Nothing is executed. Paper trading only.

05 · Predictive signals and market impact

A probability is a promise the record checks.

Research signals leave in one fixed schema: direction, horizon, stated probability, calibration bucket, provenance. When the horizon arrives the ledger scores each one against the market, hit or miss, and the stated probabilities are compared with the measured frequencies.

Signals and provenance

Every signal with its gate, its source chain and its schema version. Signals stay labelled unproven until their family earns it.

Calibration

Stated probability against measured frequency, bucket by bucket. The curve is drawn from the ledger, not from a model's own estimate.

The track record

Every emitted call scored at its horizon. Misses are kept; the chain cannot be edited.

Counted from the ledger

No scored week to draw yet (no ledger, research record or weekly pack yet).

Closer: stated against measured

Scored at the horizon, hit or miss

Every call names its horizon when it is written. When the horizon arrives it is scored against the market and the result is appended; nothing is re-scored or removed.

Measured against what was stated

A call made at 0.6 is expected to be right six times in ten. The record compares stated probabilities with measured frequencies, bucket by bucket.

The placebo beside it

Each signal family is tested against a same-name placebo in a walk-forward run. A family that does not beat its placebo is listed as such.

07 · More of the engine

Five more rooms. One tap each.

Research and reasoning, the company dossier, discovery, the portfolio and the strategy lab: what each room shows, in its own words.

Each room is a capture of the terminal on its demo dataset inside the product; here, the words.

Every conclusion keeps its evidence.

The Observatory reads one name through eight lenses and shows what it read, what it did not, and where each reading comes from. A language model may draft the words; the figures and the rules behind them are shown beside it.

  • Eight readings, one name Event, crowd, fundamentals, institutional flow, assessment, signals on record, your book, what is ahead. A node says read, not read, or nothing stored, never a guess.
  • The morning brief What changed overnight, the material events, the calendar ahead, in the order a desk would read them.

One name, the whole dossier.

Financials, valuation context, the event history, derivatives positioning and how moves propagate through the sector, on one workbench. Each figure opens a note with its definition and its source.

  • The ticker workbench Overview, research, financials, events and the model view for one company, with the stored event study beside the chart.
  • The same workbench on a phone The dossier folds into a phone without losing its sources.

Find the names worth a look, and say why they are listed.

Screens, sector maps and watchlists are the ways in. Every row says which factor put it there and on what basis; the map shades by the measured move, not by a verdict.

  • The screener Factor screens on stored bars: exposed but unmoved, moved but unexposed, chain incomplete, hedge broken, each with its basis.
  • The sector heatmap Sectors and names shaded by the day's move, and each sector against its own twenty-day average.
  • The watchlist The names you follow with their latest moves, their events ahead, and one tap to their workbench or an alert.

Know what you hold, and what can hurt it.

Import a broker statement and see exposure, concentration, the events ahead on names you hold, and how stated scenarios would move the book. Nothing is netted or smoothed without saying so.

  • Holdings and exposure Your book by sector, factor and single name, from your actual statement.
  • Risk and stress The book's move under named scenarios, with the sign convention stated on the page.
  • Theses and the frontier The stored history's best return at each level of risk, your basket against it, and the theses behind the names.

Strategies are tested before they are trusted. Paper only.

Rule-based model portfolios run as paper books; hypotheses are pre-registered and tested walk-forward against a same-name placebo; the intraday harness and the research records keep every verdict, pass or fail.

  • Strategies Rule-based model portfolios sized by a risk manager with stated caps, run on paper.
  • Experiments and backtests Pre-registered records, walk-forward verdicts, the intraday harness and the weekly pack, with their clocks.

Risk disclosure

AlgoReach provides research output: data, measurements, classifications and probabilities derived from public information. It is not investment advice, it is not tailored to your circumstances, and it does not place, route or recommend trades.

Investing in securities involves risk, including the loss of the capital invested. Probabilities on this site and in the terminal are estimates: a well-calibrated 70 percent call is still expected to be wrong about three times in ten. The record keeps every call, hits and misses alike, and the current record shows no proven edge.

Past measurements of the record do not indicate future results. Strategy and scenario figures are paper results under stated assumptions; they exclude slippage, market impact, fees and taxes unless a page says otherwise.
